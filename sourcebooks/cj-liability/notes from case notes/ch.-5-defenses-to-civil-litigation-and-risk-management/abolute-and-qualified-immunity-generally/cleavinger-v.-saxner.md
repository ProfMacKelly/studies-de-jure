# Cleavinger v. Saxner

## Issue

**Whether members of a federal a prison’s Institution Discipline Committee**, who hear cases in which inmates are charged with rules infractions, **are entitled to absolute immunity from personal liability for deprivations of constitutional rights**

## Facts

* Institution Discipline Committee found a group of prison inmates **guilty of encouraging a work stoppage and other misconduct; punishment: held in administrative detention and forfeit of "good time"**
* Prisoner appeal to Warden and Reg. Dir. of the Bureau of Prisons: Prisoners released and record of incident expunged
* Later paroled and released, but **before release, they filed suit in fed ct against the Discipline Committee members for constitutional violation**s
* Trial - Jury found violations of 5th Amend DP rights and awarded damages
  * On appeal - petitioners claim absolute immunity, analogizing their function to a judicial function

## Law

* **Judicial immunity longstanding**
  * Protect integrity of judicial process



* Absolute immunity extended to **certain others who perform functions closely associated with the judicial process**
  * Fed hearing examiners/ALJs
  * Fed/state prosecutors
  * Witnesses (incl. police officers testifying in judicial proceedings) - integral part of the judicial process
  * Grand jurors
* State parole officials
* Presidential "official acts"



* Immunity **based on function, not status** of the def (i.e., not rank, title, or location in gov't); it is based on nature of the responsibilities of the individual official
  * **Factors:**
    1. the need to assure that the individual can perform his functions without harassment or intimidation;&#x20;
    2. the presence of safeguards that reduce the need for private damages actions as a means of controlling unconstitutional conduct;&#x20;
    3. insulation from political influence;&#x20;
    4. the importance of precedent;&#x20;
    5. the adversary nature of the process; and&#x20;
    6. the correctability of error on appeal
  * Must balance the public interest vs. individual interest

## Analysis

* **adjudicatory function** of the Committee:
  * determine whether the accused inmate is guilty or innocent of the charge leveled against him;&#x20;
  * hear testimony and receive documentary evidence;&#x20;
  * evaluate credibility and weigh evidence;&#x20;
  * render a decision
* **some societal importance** in this dispute-resolution function - administration of a prison is a difficult undertaking bc it concerns persons many of whom have demonstrated a proclivity for antisocial, criminal, and violent conduct
* many inmates do not refrain from harassment and intimidation; possible retaliation
* **Committee members are usually of modest means** if personally liable
* <mark style="color:$danger;">**But - Function**</mark>
  * Not a professional function
  * Employees of the Bureau of Prisons
  * Working with fellow employees to lodge complaints against inmates (thus pressures about outcomes)
  * Not the same function as parole board:
    * a neutral and detached hearing body
    * judicial role as an arm of sentencing judge
    * constitutionally required to provide DP protections (more than Committee)
  * Committee more analagous to school bd membership
    * adjudicators within an administrative disciplinary process
    * absolute immunity wouldn't sufficiently increase ability to exercise discretion in a forthright manner to warrant an absence of a remedy for violations of an individ's rights
    * But - school bd members are open to more public scrutiny than Committee members, which have closed-door procedures
* **Prisoners have few procedural safeguards:**
  * no lawyer or representative
  * no right to compel witnesses or cross-examine witnesses
  * no right to discovery
  * no burden of proof
  * no transcript/record
  * no evidentiary restrictions (e.g., hearsay) or protection against self-serving testimony
  * no true independent decision-maker
  * Expensive for the gov't?
    * frivolous litigation quickly disposed of through Rules of Civil Procedure
      * few go beyond a complaint
        * most are pro se, so little disovery or other gov't resources spend defending the cases
      * Gov't has alternatives:
        * indemnifying the officials
        * Congress - apply these claims FTCA; institution of ALJs
  * Conflict between maintaining order (the Committee's/prison's role) and protecting rights
