# Anderson v. Creighton

## Facts

Anderson (FBI agent) participated with other law enforcement officers in a warrantless search of Creighton's home. The search was conducted because petitioner believed that one Dixon, who was suspected of a bank robbery committed earlier that day, might be found there, but he was not.&#x20;

Respondents filed a state court action, asserting a claim for damages under the Fourth Amendment.

Fed Trial Ct dismissed on MSJ saying the lawfulness of the search couldn't have been decided

Ct. App. reversed - MSJ inappropriate because of factual disputes bearing on the constitutionality of the search, and if unconstitutional then qualified immunity wouldn't apply

## Issue

whether a federal law enforcement officer who participates in a search that violates the Fourth Amendment may be held personally liable for money damages if a reasonable officer could have believed that the search comported with the Fourth Amendment

## Holding

qualified immunity grounds if he can establish as a matter of law that a reasonable officer could have believed that the search comported with the Fourth Amendment, even though it actually did not

## Rule

Whether an official protected by qualified immunity may be held personally liable for an allegedly unlawful official action generally turns on:

* "objective legal reasonableness" of the action, assessed in light of the legal rules that were "**clearly established" at the time the action was taken**
  * **Determining "clearly established," the contours of the right must be sufficiently clear that a reasonable official would understand that what he is doing violates that right**
* The relevant question is **whether a reasonable officer could have believed the warrantless search to be lawful, in light of clearly established law and the information the searching officers possessed**. (The officer's subjective beliefs about the search are irrelevant.)

## Discussion

* it is **inevitable that law enforcement officials will in some cases reasonably but mistakenly conclude that probable cause is present**, and we have indicated that in such cases those officials — like other officials who act in ways they **reasonably believe to be lawful — should not be held personally liable**. See [_Ma_](#user-content-fn-1)[^1]_lley_. The **same is true of their conclusions regarding exigent circumstances**.
