# Procunier v. Navarette (1978)

## Facts

* Navarette - inmate @ state prison. Defs were director of Dept. of Corrections, warden, and asst. warden, and 3 subordinate officers who directly handled inmate mail
*   Navarette claims prison officials **interfered with his outgoing mail** over a 15-month period—allegedly ...

    * Letters were:
      * Not mailed at all
      * Returned to him
      * Not sent via registered mail when requested
      * Never received by recipients
    * Intended recipients included:
      * Legal assistance groups
      * Law students
      * Media
      * Other inmates
      * Personal contacts

    👉 This matters because correspondence with these groups implicates **First Amendment expression and access to courts**.





*   Claims - wrongful interference with mail, failure to mail letters (including those to legal assistance personal, law students, media, inmates at other prisons, and personal friends)

    —>&#x20;

    * **First Amendment** → free speech (sending letters)
    * **Fifth Amendment** → due process (federal dimension, though typically incorporated)
    * **Fourteenth Amendment** → due process (applies to state actors)

    —> Alleg:&#x20;
* §§ 1983 & 1985

**Claim: Intentional misconduct**

* Officials acted in **“knowing disregard”** of:
  * Prison regulations
  * Constitutional rights
* Supervisors:
  * **Condoned the conduct**
  * **Conspired** with subordinates

**Claim: Bad faith conduct**

* Same underlying facts (mail interference)
* But framed as:
  * **“Bad faith disregard”** (requires malicious or improper motive)

**Claim: Negligence**

* Officials acted:
  * **“Negligently and inadvertently”**
* Supervisors:
  * Failed to properly **train or supervise**
* **Negligence alone typically does NOT establish a constitutional violation under §1983**
* Courts usually require:
  * **Intentional conduct** or
  * **Deliberate indifference**
* Supervisory/Vicarious Liability
  * Personal involvement, OR
  * Deliberate indifference, OR
  * Participation in a policy or custom

Trial court: Dismissal based on qualified immunity from liability for damages under § 1983.&#x20;

Ct. App.: Reversed, holding that prisoners are entitled to First and Fourteenth Amendment protection for their outgoing mail - triable issue&#x20;

## Issue

Both bad-faith and negligence in handling the mail

_Held:_ The Court of Appeals erred in reversing the District Court's summary judgment for petitioners. Pp. [434 U. S. 560](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/434/555/#560)-566.

## **Rule**

* &#x20;prison officials and officers =  not absolutely immune \[note: compare with standards used in _Cleavinger]_; In _Scheuer v. Rhodes ..._
  * Absolute vs. qualifed depends on&#x20;
    * scope of discretion and responsibilities of the office; AND
    * and all the circumstances as they reasonably appeared at the time of the action
  * Qualified:
    * Reasonable grounds for the belief formed at the time and in light of the circumstances AND
    * good-faith belief
* State prison officials entitled to immunity unless they:
  * "knew or reasonably should have known" that the actions they took would violate his federal constitutional rights; **OR**
  * took the actions with the "malicious intention" to cause a deprivation of constitutional rights or other injury (_Wood v. Strickland,_ [420 U. S. 308](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/420/308/), [420 U. S. 32](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/420/31/#32). Pp. [434 U. S. 561](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/434/555/#561)-562.)
* Officials cannot be expected to predict the future course of constitutional law ... BUT
  * not be shielded from liability if they act “with such disregard of the \[plaintiff's] clearly established constitutional rights that the action cannot reasonably be characterized as being in good faith.”



* _**Strickland**_**&#x20;Rule Part 1**

1. At the time of the official's conduct that allegedly infringed upon a constitutional right, what that right clearly established?
2. If 1 is yes, did they know or should they have known of that right?
3. If 2 is yes,  did they know or should they have known that the conduct would violate the right?

* _**Strickland**_**&#x20;Rule Part 2**
  * "malicious intention" to cause a deprivation of constitutional rights or other injury



## Discussion

#### 1. No Clearly Established Right

* At the time (1971–1972), **no clearly established First or Fourteenth Amendment right** protected prisoner mail in this context.
* Officials cannot be expected to anticipate future constitutional developments.
* Therefore, they could not have known their conduct violated a constitutional right.

#### 2. Negligence Is Insufficient

* The claim before the Court was **purely negligence-based**.
* Qualified immunity is only defeated by:
  * Knowledge of illegality, or
  * **Malicious intent**
* Negligence:
  * Implies lack of intent
  * Does **not** satisfy the malicious intent requirement

#### 3. Policy Considerations

* Officials must be able to perform duties **without fear of liability** for reasonable mistakes.
* Imposing liability for negligence would undermine discretionary decision-making.

***

### **Disposition**

Judgment of the Court of Appeals **reversed**; summary judgment for defendants reinstated.

***

### **Key Takeaways / Doctrinal Significance**

* **Qualified immunity protects against negligence claims** under § 1983.
* Liability requires:
  * Violation of a **clearly established right**, or
  * **Intentional/malicious conduct**
* The case reinforces:
  * The **objective legal standard** for “clearly established law”
  * The distinction between **negligence vs. constitutional torts**
* Important for:
  * Prisoner rights litigation
  * Civil rights suits against government officials
