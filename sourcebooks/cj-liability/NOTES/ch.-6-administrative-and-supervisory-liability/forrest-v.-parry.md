# Forrest v. Parry

## Facts

* Forrest visited an acquaintance's house.&#x20;
* Police kicked in the door, and Forrest went upstairs to Blevins's bedroom to inform Blevins. Police kick in the bedroom door. At some point, Officer Parry hit Forrest, knocking him out; after regaining consciousness, Parry repeatedly hit him in the face, handcuffed him, and dragged him down the stairs, causing lacerations, bruising, and injuries to his knees.
* After being put in the supervising Sergant's (Morris) vehicle, Morris took Forrest to a vacant lot and told Forrest that his officers don't plan drugs on people. Then, two officers arrived, Forrest was moved to the officers' vehicle and taken to the hospital for treatment.&#x20;
* At the hospital, Forrest lied about the reason for the injuries; previously officers warned him that if he told the truth, they would charge him with assaulting 5 officers.
* In the police report, Officer Parry falsely reported Forrest's involvement in a drug deal and being assaulted by Forrest; Parry further falsely testified against Forrest to a grand jury. Forrest later pleaded guilty with possession with intent, serving 18 months. \[After release from prison, Parry admitted to his falsifications. Parry, Morris, and another officer were charged with, and pleaded guilty to, conspiracy to deprive others of their civil rights.]
* Forrest filed complaint with IA; no response
* While in prison, Forrest brought a 1983 action against the city of Camden, conspiracy uner 1985, and a state law claim for negligent supervision
  * Allegations:&#x20;
    * City has policy or custom of permitting officers to be "essential unsupervised" and was the "moving force" behind constitutional deprivations
    * City's failure to train and supervise officers constituted deliberate indifference
* State AG's office had previously reviewed the city's operations 5 times before Forrest's arrest. One report warned that the city's failure to commit manpower and resources--and the backlog of over 350 uninvestigated complaints of police misconduct--would place it “in the position of failing to adequately protect the civil rights of its citizens and sets the stage for significant civil liability.”&#x20;
* Camden never addressed the backlog, which went as high as 487 complaints.
* The investigations themselves were cursory and otherwise "seriously deficient"
* Admissions by former PD officials that there were no performance reviews and not enough supervisors per officers in the time leading up to Forrest's arrest; there was no scrutiny of what went officer reports
* Officers admitted that supervisors didn't seem to care what the officers did

## **Issue**

What is sufficient evidence from which a jury can infer that a municipality adopted a custom of permitting its police officers to use excessive force?

## **Holding**

\[Internal Affairs Unit (“Internal Affairs”) of the since-disbanded Camden Police Department was woefully deficient in investigating civilian complaints about officer misconduct]

## **Rule**

2 ways to pursue a 1983 claim against a municipality:[ **(1) A plaintiff may put forth that an unconstitutional policy or custom of the municipality led to his or her injuries**](#user-content-fn-1)[^1]

**(2) that the injuries were caused by a failure or inadequacy by the municipality that “reflects a deliberate or conscious choice”**&#x20;

**Failure to Train, supervision, discipline and others fall under the second option**

**If pursuing the "policy or custom" route:**

* A plaintiff presenting an unconstitutional policy **must point to an official proclamation, policy or edict by a decisionmaker possessing final authority to establish municipal policy on the relevant subject.**&#x20;
* **If alleging a custom, the plaintiff must evince a given course of conduct so well-settled and permanent as to virtually constitute law**.

**If pursuing claims on failures/inadequacies:**

* A plaintiff must **demonstrate a failure or inadequacy that amounts to deliberate indifference on the part of the municipality**.&#x20;
* The alleged deficiencies **must be closely related to the alleged constitutional injury**
  * **Must show whether: (1)** municipal policymakers know that employees will confront a particular situation, **(2)** the situation involves a difficult choice or a history of employees mishandling, and **(3)** the wrong choice by an employee will frequently cause deprivation of constitutional rights.&#x20;



## **Analysis**

**???Failure to Supervise - Municipality???**

* Camden policymaker's knew officers needed supervision
  * **History of officer supervision being mishandled**, thus constitutional violations were likely to continue without supervision
  * **Evidence of specific officers repeated engaging in false arrest and excessive force** while knowing they weren't being supervised, and some of the incidents **should have alerted their superiors**
  * **Evidence of IA complaint backlog and testimony of leadership personnel** \[**EXPAND**] enough to create an issue of fact
    * Even in **investigations, discipline wasn't occurring**

**Failure to Train**

**Training Program.** Even assuming that with the volume of complaints that Camden had to have known of the problems of officers violating citizens' rights, **the link between the alleged training program deficiencies and the alleged violation is too weak**

* The officers **knew that their conduct was criminal, and, as the encounter in this case shows, used their authority to pressure victims** to refrain from immediately reporting their activities
* **Can't simply infer that training program changes would have made a difference** in Forrest's alleged injuries.&#x20;
* In terms of awareness, **the testimonial evidence from higher officials point to supervision and accountability as the critical issues, not training**.

**Failure to Supervise**

* **policymakers knew or should have known that supervisor-level officers would be confronted with officer misconduc**t, whether firsthand or via complaints and reports from others, and that the wrong choice—**failure to report or admonish—would lead to the sort of behavior that occurred here: officers whose behavior caused the deprivation of constitutional rights, but who had no reason to change that behavior**.
* the evidence here demonstrates a **genuine dispute of material fact as to whether supervisors had a history of mishandling**
* **sheer volume of complaints from outsiders, coupled with the absence of any internal response&#x20;**<mark style="color:$danger;">**may lead a reasonable jury to conclude that Camden was aware of supervisors mishandling or being unable to handle their duties**</mark>. This is even more pronounced when one examines the testimonies of <mark style="color:$danger;">**higher officials who expressed great concern that officers were not being adequately supervised, and called for various measures to address that reality**</mark>**,** including a formal performance evaluation system and a reduction in the supervisor-to-officer ratio. _See also_ App. 128 (warning that Camden’s failure to commit manpower and resources to proactively managing police misconduct would place it “in the position of failing to adequately protect the civil rights of its citizens and set the stage for significant civil liability.”)
  * **The call for these measures was warranted and the need for training was apparent**. The **testimony provided** by Officers Stetser and Parry reflects that t**hey were aware that supervision was lacking,** whether co-conspirator Sergeant Morris covered for them or not. Officer Stetser, **in particular, explained that one of his supervisors “most likely” knew that he was writing false reports, and accepted them**. \[...] The record further provides ample basis for this confidence.&#x20;
    * Recall that Officer Stetser failed an integrity test administered by a supervising officer, and pranked another by planting drugs in the supervising officer’s bag. When this was reported to a Sergeant in Internal Affairs, the Sergeant merely responded with his own account of similar behavior by Officer Stetser in other contexts.&#x20;
    * The foregoing demonstrates Camden’s policymakers were aware that Camden needed a large shake up in its supervisory regime. I**t also raises significant questions as to whether Camden’s supervisor-level officers were adequately trained on how to discipline and combat officer misconduct when it was brought to their attention, including the kinds of misconduct—false arrest and excessive force—that led to Forrest’s injuries.**&#x20;
      * Thus, **while we agree that Forrest’s claim regarding the adequacy of the training officers received fails on causation grounds, we conclude that a genuine dispute of material fact exists as to whether the need for more or different training for supervisors was obvious, and the failure to provide that was very likely to result in a violation of constitutional rights**. We will therefore reverse the District Court’s summary judgment ruling as to this iteration of Forrest’s § 1983 claim.

**Jury Instructions**

Two errors: first, the instructions confuse the jury as to the legal requirements for each species of § 1983 liability, and, second, it narrows the jury’s focus to only evidence pertaining to Internal Affairs and Officers Stetser and Parry.

**First** - the jury here was i**ncorrectly instructed tha**t, in order to find a municipal liability for i**nadequate supervision, it had to find that Camden adopted a policy or custom of inadequate supervision amounting to deliberate indifference to the fact that it would “obviously result in the violation** of an individual’s right to be free from unlawful arrest and excessive force.”

* may lead to **confusion over whether deliberate indifference is required for the "policy or custom" option for municipality liability**

**Second** - rather than the adequacy of Camden’s supervision and investigation of its officers in general, the instruction frames the case as solely related to the supervision of officers involved in Forrest's case. Specifically, the instructions state that,

> In order to hold the municipality liable for the violation of \[Forrest’s constitutional rights] ..., you must find that \[Forrest] has ... proved by preponderance of the evidence ... \[that] \[f]irst, \[Internal Affairs] failed to adequately supervise _Stetser_ and _Parry_. Second, \[Internal Affairs]’s failure to supervise _Stetser_ and _Parry_ amounted to deliberate indifference .... Third, \[Internal Affairs]’s failure to adequately supervise\[ ] proximately cause\[d] the violation ...

**Further**, in instructing the **jury on the elements of deliberate indifference, the Court again directed the jury to examine whether “\[Internal Affairs] knew that Jason Stetser and Kevin Parry would confront a particular situation.”**&#x20;

* the **legal requirement for deliberate indifference is whether “(1) municipal policymakers know that&#x20;**_**employees**_**&#x20;will confront a particular situation; (2) the situation involves a difficult choice or a history of employees mishandling; and (3) the wrong choice by an employee will frequently cause deprivation of constitutional rights.”**
* It is not narrowed to the particular employees in the case. Notably, **as the record makes clear, the Chief of Police had ultimate authority over Camden’s police department and Internal Affairs but is not properly considered within Internal Affairs. W**e therefore conclude that the instructions provided to the jury regarding Forrest’s § 1983 claim constituted error.
