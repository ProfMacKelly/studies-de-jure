#### A Mathematical Syllogism Analogy

For some people, an analogy to mathematics may be helpful. Many mathematical calculations also proceed from general to specific propositions:

> **Question:** What is the length of the hypothenuse—side 'c'—of right triangle in which the other legs are three feet and four feet long?
>
> **Major Premise** (the formula[^1])**:** Where the hypothenuse of a right triangle is 'c' and the legs of the lengths of the triangle's legs are 'a' and 'b,' then:&#x20;

$$
c =\sqrt({a}^2 + b^2)
$$

adf

>
>
> **Minor Premise(s)** (the variables)**:**&#x20;
>
> * Side 'a' is 3 feet long.&#x20;
> * Side 'b' is 4 feet long.
>
> **Analysis:**
>
> * 'a' squared equals 9 feet.
> * 'b' squared equals 16 feet.
> * 'a' squared plus 'b' squared equal 25 feet.
> * The square root of 25 feet is 5 feet.
>
> **Conclusion:** The hypotenuse ('c') of the right triangle is 5 feet.

As you can see, to reach the logical conclusion, we (1) start with a general proposition (i.e., the formula or rule relevant to evaluate the problem under the given set of facts); (2) apply the general proposition to the secondary propositions (i.e., the variables or facts given); and (3) reach the conclusion.

{% hint style="info" %}
As we will see, applying a mathematical formula to a set of variables is not a perfect analogy syllogistic or deductive reasoning in the law. A substantive distinction is that in mathematics, a theorem or formula serving as a major premise [can be proven true or false](#user-content-fn-2)[^2]; in the law, however, a legal "rule" is a standard for governing or discerning someone's legal rights and obligations, which may change over time due to actions of the courts, legislatures, and government agencies.
{% endhint %}
