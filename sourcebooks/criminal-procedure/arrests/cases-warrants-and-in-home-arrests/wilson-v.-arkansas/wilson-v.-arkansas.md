# Wilson v. Arkansas

## Wilson v. Arkansas (1995)

Context: At time of the Constitution's framing, common law instructed LE to announce presence and authority before entering a home to make an arrest - the "knock and announce" principle

Facts:

* Sharlene Wilson made series of MJ and meth sales at her home to an informant working with Arkansas State Police; another person, Bryson Jacobs, was with Wilson
* Following day, police obtained warrants to search Wilson's home and arrest her; the affidavit includes the drug sale details and stated that Jacobs had prior convictions for arson and firebombing
* When conducting the search that afternoon, officers found the main door to the house open, but an unlocked screen door was closed
* Officered opened the screen door and entered, identified themselves as police and that they had a warrant
* Seized MJ, meth, other drugs and paraphernalia, a gun, and ammunition; also found Wilson flushing MJ down the toilet
* Wilson alleged that the search was invalid because officers didn't knock and announce before entering

I: Whether the knock and announce principle is relevant to the Fourth Amendment reasonableness test

H: Yes

Common law knock and announce principle is part of the test for reasonableness under the 4th Amend; depending on the circumstances, unannounced entry can be unreasonable

**But**, LE has countervailing interests (e.g.):

threat of physical harm to police

the fact that an officer is pursuing a recently escaped arrestee

the existence of reason to believe that evidence would likely be destroyed if advance notice were given

Here, the police may have reasonably believed that knock and announce would have placed them in peril and would have produced an unreasonable risk that Wilson would easily destroy disposable narcotics evidence

(Remanded back to state court for reasonableness determinations)
