# Automobile - Knowles

(unanimous)

An Iowa policeman stopped petitioner Knowles for speeding and issued him a **citation** rather than arresting him. The officer then conducted a full search of the car, without either Knowles' consent or probable cause, found marijuana and a "pot pipe," and arrested Knowles. Before his trial on state drug charges, Knowles moved to suppress the evidence, arguing that because he had not been arrested, the search could not be sustained under the "search incident to arrest" exception recognized in _United States_ v. _Robinson_

* Did the justifications (stated by _Chimel_ Court) for performing a search incident to lawful arrest justify the search of Knowles?
  * No.&#x20;
* Depending on your answer to the previous question, explain why or why not?
  * The threat to officer safety from issuing a traffic citation is a good deal less than in the case of a custodial arrest. While concern for safety during a routine traffic stop may justify ordering a driver and passengers out of the car, it does not by itself justify the often considerably greater intrusion attending a full fieldtype search;
    * Officers need an independent bases to search for weapons and protect themselves from danger
  * The need to discover and preserve evidence does not exist in a traffic stop, for once Knowles was stopped for speeding and issued a citation, all evidence necessary to prosecute that offense had been obtained
    * The possibility that an officer would stumble onto evidence of an unrelated offense seems remote
