# Illinois v. Gates

## Facts

### Tip & Activities

* May 3, 1978 - PD of Bloomingdale, IL, received an anonymous letter which included statements that:
  * Respondents, husband and wife, were engaged in selling drugs;
    * The wife would <mark style="background-color:$warning;">drive their car to Florida on May 3</mark> to be loaded with drugs, and the husband would fly down in a few days to drive the car back;
    * The car's trunk would be loaded with drugs;
    * Respondents presently had over $100,000 worth of drugs in their basement.&#x20;
* Acting on the tip, a police officer determined respondents' address and learned that the <mark style="background-color:$warning;">husband made a reservation on a May 5 flight to Florida</mark>.
* Arrangements for surveillance of the flight were made with an agent of the Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA):
  * Husband took the flight, stayed overnight in a <mark style="background-color:$warning;">motel room registered in the wife's name</mark>
  * Husband left the following morning <mark style="background-color:$warning;">with a woman in a car bearing an Illinois license plate issued to the husband,</mark> heading north on an interstate highway <mark style="background-color:$warning;">used by travelers to the Bloomingdale</mark> area.

### **Warrant & PC Determination**

* <mark style="background-color:$warning;">A search</mark> <mark style="background-color:$warning;"></mark><mark style="background-color:$warning;">**warrant for respondents' residence and automobile**</mark> was then obtained from an Illinois state court judge, **based on the Bloomingdale police officer's affidavit setting forth the foregoing facts and a copy of the anonymous letter**.&#x20;
* When respondents arrived at their home, the police were waiting and **discovered marihuana and other contraband in respondents' car trunk and home**.&#x20;
* Prior to respondents' trial on charges of violating state drug laws, the trial court ordered suppression of all the items seized, and the Illinois Appellate Court affirmed.&#x20;
  * **The Illinois Supreme Court also affirmed, holding that the letter and affidavit were inadequate to sustain a determination of probable cause f**or issuance of the search warrant since they failed to satisfy the **"two-pronged test" (which the court interpreted&#x20;**_**Aguilar v. Texas**_**&#x20;and&#x20;**_**Spinelli v. U.S.**_**&#x20;to require both):(1) revealing the informant's "basis of knowledge" \[i.e., establishing that the informant is reliable] and (2) providing sufficient facts to establish the informant's "veracity" and the "reliability" of the informant's report.**

## Issue

Did there need to be both reliability of informant's information AND informant's basis of knowledge to establish PC?

## Holding

No.

## **Rule**

* PC must be determined by the **totality of the circumstances too fluid to apply a technical rule (like requiring both the prongs to be satisfied)**
* PC turns on the assessment of **probabilities in particular factual contexts**
* The 2 prongs are relevant considerations, but **both do not need to be satisfied**:
  * **Basis of the informant's knowledge \[i.e., establishing that the informant is reliable], and**
  * **Sufficient facts to establish the informant's "veracity" and the "reliability" of the informant's report**

(Being too technical or formulaic can seriously impeded law enforcement)

## Analysis

&#x20;**Public policy reasons:** In practice, informant tips are so varying that they can't be reduced to a scholarly construct&#x20;

* In everyday life, **witnesses generally don't provide extensive explanations of the basis of their everyday observations, so many anonymous tips wouldn't pass the test** thereby reducing the usefulness of tips
* **Anonymous tips have great value**, and "anonymous tips seldom could survive a rigorous application of either of the _Spinelli_ prong"

\[**Based on the totality of the circumstances**--review for the details]&#x20;

* Fair probability that the writer of the anonymous letter had obtained his entire story either from the Gateses or someone they trusted.&#x20;
  * Corroboration of major portions of the letter’s predictions.&#x20;
  * Thus, the judge issuing the warrant had a “substantial basis for … conclud\[ing]” that probable cause to search the Gateses’ home and car existed.&#x20;
