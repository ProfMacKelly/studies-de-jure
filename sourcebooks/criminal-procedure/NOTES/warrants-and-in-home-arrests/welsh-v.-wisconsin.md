# Welsh v. Wisconsin

## Facts

A witness observed a car that was being driven erratically and that eventually swerved off the road, coming to a stop in a field without causing damage to any person or property. Ignoring the witness' suggestion that he wait for assistance in removing his car, the driver walked away from the scene.

The police arrived a few minutes later, and were told by the witness that the driver was either very inebriated or very sick. After checking the car's registration, the police, without obtaining a warrant, proceeded to the petitioner's nearby home, arriving at about 9 p.m.

They gained entry when petitioner's stepdaughter answered the door, and found petitioner lying naked in bed.

Petitioner was then arrested for driving a motor vehicle while under the influence of an intoxicant in violation of a Wisconsin statute which provided that a first offense was a noncriminal violation subject to a civil forfeiture proceeding for a maximum fine of $200. Petitioner was taken to the police station, where he refused to submit to a breath analysis test.

Pursuant to Wisconsin statutes, which subjected an arrestee who refused to take the test to the risk of a 60-day revocation of driving privileges, petitioner requested a court hearing to determine whether his refusal was reasonable

(Stepdaughter's consent wasn't considered; trial court never evaluated it because it decided that exigent circumstances applied)

Trial court found the arrest lawful and issued an order suspending Petitioner's license

Ct. App. reversed on the basis that the warrantless arrest was unconstitutional because the police didn't establish the existence of exigent circumstances

## Issue

Whether, and if so under what circumstances, the Fourth Amendment prohibits the police from making a warrantless night entry of a person’s home in order to arrest him for a nonjailable traffic offense.

## Holding

This night entry was unconstiutional

## &#x20;Rule

&#x20;Before government agents may invade the sanctity of the home, the government must demonstrate exigent circumstances that overcome the presumption of unreasonableness that attaches to all warrantless home entries

* Exigency is not created merely by the existence of PC
* A key factors in determining the existence of an exigency: Gravity of the underlying offense for which the arrest is being made

## Analysis

The nature of this offense was too minor to justify warrantless entry

No other exigencies, such as:

* hot pursuit - no immediate or continuous purpose from scene of crime or threat to public safety (he was already home and left his car)
* preventing destruction of evidence -&#x20;
  * the need to preserve this evidence (minimize dissipation of blood alcohol level) vs. the significance of the intrusion under the circumstance

&#x20;

DISSENT - Blackmun complaining about the failure of states to have harsher penalties for drunk driving.

* "But if Wisconsin and other States choose by legislation thus to regulate their penalty structure, there is, unfortunately, nothing in the United States Constitution that says they may not do so"&#x20;
