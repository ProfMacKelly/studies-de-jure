# Terry v. Ohio

## **Issue**

Broadly: Is it is always unreasonable for a policeman to seize a person and subject him to a limited search for weapons unless there is probable cause for an arrest?

{% hint style="info" %}
**4th Amend:**&#x20;

* The <mark style="background-color:orange;">**right of the people to be secure in their persons**</mark>, houses, papers, and effects, against <mark style="background-color:orange;">**unreasonable searches and seizures,**</mark> shall not be violated, <mark style="background-color:orange;">**and no Warrants shall issue, but upon probable cause, supported by Oath or affirmation, and particularly describing the place to be searched, and the persons or things to be seized**</mark>.
{% endhint %}

## Holding

No.

## Rule

**Carefully limited** search of the **outer clothing** is permitted when:

* Officer observes unusual conduct which leads him **reasonably to conclude ...**
  * in light of experience that criminal activity may be afoot and&#x20;
  * that the persons with whom he is dealing may be armed and presently dangerous
* &#x20;In the course of investigating this behavior ...&#x20;
  * the officer identifies themselves as an officer&#x20;
  * makes reasonable inquiries, and&#x20;
  * where nothing in the initial stages of the encounter serves to dispel a reasonable fear for own or others’ safety
* Purpose is to protect themselves and others in the area&#x20;
  * to discover weapons which might be used for harm
* The officer is in close range of the suspect

When an officer is justified in believing that the individual whose suspicious behavior he is investigating at close range is armed and presently dangerous to the officer or to others, it would appear to be clearly unreasonable to deny the officer the power to take necessary measures to determine whether the person is in fact armed and dangerous

And in justifying the particular intrusion the police officer must be able to point to specific and articulable facts which, taken together with rational inferences from those facts, reasonably warrant that intrusion.&#x20;

## Analysis

**Public Policy: Balancing of Interests**

| Law Enforcement & Public                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         | Individual                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Effective Crime Prevention: The need to search or seize**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | **Protection against unreasonable invasions**                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| <ul><li>Situations, often dangerous, rapidly unfold on city streets, so the police need of an escalating set of flexible responses</li><li>Interest of the police officer in taking steps to assure himself that the person with whom he is dealing is not armed with a weapon that could unexpectedly and fatally be used against him</li></ul> | <ul><li>Authority of policy must be constrained by the law of arrest and search</li><li>Even a limited search of the outer clothing for weapons constitutes a <strong>severe</strong>, though brief, <strong>intrusion upon cherished personal security</strong>, and it must surely be an annoying, frightening, and perhaps humiliating experience.</li></ul> |

* Need practical rule because:
  * Must consider the realities of the many situations involving police-citizen interactions
  * A variety of police activity necessarily goes beyond voluntary/consensual interactions _but are less than_ what constitutes an arrest based on PC
    * Need to distinguish between a “stop” and an “arrest” (or a “seizure” of a person), and between a “frisk” and a “search.”
    * And based on the realities, we can't require PC for everything more than voluntary/consensual interactions
* Must test based on reasonableness - depends on the  the amount of information they possess
  * Necessarily swift actions of officers predicated upon the on-the-spot observations which historically have not been, and as a practical matter could not be, subjected to the warrant procedure
* Because of the nature/quality of the intrusion: There must be a **narrowly drawn authority** to permit a reasonable search for weapons for the protection of the police officer&#x20;
  * where there is reason to believe the person armed and dangerous individual, regardless of whether PC exists to arrest for a crime
  * imperative that the facts be judged against **an objective standard: Would the facts available to the officer at the moment of the seizure or the search** “warrant a man of **reasonable caution** in the belief” that the action taken was appropriate?&#x20;
    * Cannot be subjective
      * A simple “good faith" test would cause the protections of the Fourth Amendment would evaporate because we'd be left only with discretion of the police.
* So: Would a reasonably prudent man in the circumstances would be warranted in the belief that his safety or that of others was in danger
  * Whether the officer acted reasonably in such circumstances, due weight must be given, **not to his inchoate and unparticularized suspicion or “hunch,”** but **to the specific reasonable inferences which he is entitled to draw from the facts in light of his experience**
  * Frisk is **not justified by any need to prevent the disappearance or destruction of evidence of crime**.&#x20;
    * Sole justification of the search: Protection of the police officer and others nearby
    * Must be confined in scope to an intrusion reasonably designed to discover guns, knives, clubs, or other hidden instruments for the assault of the police officer.

**Application to the Facts**

The crux of this case: Whether there was justification for McFadden’s invasion of Terry’s personal security by searching him for weapons in the course of that investigation

1. At what point in this encounter the Fourth Amendment becomes relevant. That is, **we must decide whether and when Officer McFadden “seized” Terry and whether and when he conducted a “search.”**

No question that Officer McFadden “seized” petitioner and subjected him to a “search” when he took hold of him and patted down the outer surfaces of his clothing.&#x20;

2. **Whether at that point it was reasonable for Officer McFadden to have interfered with petitioner’s personal security as he did."**

* Experience, training, observations of conduct made it reasonable to believe ...
  * Based on the suspected crime involved, further reasonable they may be armed and dangerous
* McFadden patted down the outer clothing&#x20;
* He did not place his hands in their pockets or under the outer surface of their garments until he had felt weapons, and&#x20;
* He merely reached for and removed the guns.
