# U.S. v. Sharpe

More on the permissible scope of investigatory stops based on reasonable suspicion. In particular, it helps to illustrate how long a person may be detained for a “_Terry_ stop.”

***

**Facts:**

* **Initial Observations and Suspicion:**
  * Agent Cooke, a DEA agent patrolling a known drug trafficking corridor in South Carolina, noticed a heavily loaded pickup truck with a camper shell. The truck's low-riding posture was a key indicator to Cooke, based on his experience, that it was carrying a substantial load.
  * Simultaneously, a Pontiac Bonneville was observed traveling in tandem with the pickup. This coordinated travel pattern heightened Cooke's suspicion, as it is a common tactic used by drug traffickers.
  * The Pontiac, driven by William Sharpe, began to exhibit evasive driving maneuvers. These actions included sudden changes in speed and attempts to distance itself from Agent Cooke's unmarked vehicle.
  * When Sharpe made an abrupt turn onto a side road, Cooke's suspicion solidified, leading him to believe that the vehicles were involved in illegal activity.
* **The Simultaneous Stops:**
  * Agent Cooke, recognizing the need for assistance, radioed for a marked patrol car. Officer Thrasher responded to the call.
  * Cooke initiated a stop of the Pontiac, while Thrasher pursued and stopped the pickup truck, driven by Donald Davis, approximately half a mile further down the road. This concurrent stopping allowed for the detention of both vehicles.
  * During the initial encounter with Sharpe, Cooke requested his driver's license and vehicle registration. Sharpe was unable to produce a vehicle registration, adding to the suspicion.
  * While interacting with Sharpe, Cooke detected the distinct odor of marijuana emanating from the Pontiac. This sensory evidence further reinforced his belief that the vehicle contained contraband.
  * Upon opening the Pontiac's trunk, Cooke visually confirmed the presence of large, tightly wrapped bales of marijuana. Sharpe was immediately placed under arrest.
* **The Delayed Search of the Pickup Truck:**
  * Officer Thrasher maintained control of Davis and the pickup truck during the period that Cooke was dealing with Sharpe.
  * Cooke, after securing Sharpe and the Pontiac, proceeded to the location of the pickup truck.
  * The elapsed time between the initial stop of Sharpe and Cooke's arrival at the pickup truck was approximately 20 minutes. This period was the central point of contention in the legal arguments.
  * Upon arriving at the pickup truck, Cooke, with Thrasher's assistance, opened the rear of the camper shell.
  * Inside, they discovered numerous bales of marijuana, mirroring the contents of Sharpe's Pontiac. Davis was then arrested.

**Procedural History:**

* **District Court Level:**
  * Sharpe and Davis were indicted on charges of possession of marijuana with intent to distribute.
  * Their defense attorneys filed motions to suppress the evidence, asserting that the investigatory stops were unlawful and that the duration of Davis's detention was unreasonably prolonged.
  * The District Court conducted an evidentiary hearing, where the officers testified about the circumstances of the stops and searches.
  * The court, after considering the evidence, ruled that the officers had reasonable suspicion to initiate the stops and that the 20-minute detention of Davis was not unreasonable in light of the circumstances.
  * Sharpe and Davis were subsequently tried and convicted.
* **Court of Appeals (Fourth Circuit):**
  * Sharpe and Davis appealed their convictions to the Fourth Circuit Court of Appeals.
  * The appellate court focused on the length of Davis's detention, specifically the 20-minute period.
  * **The&#x20;**<mark style="color:red;">**Fourth Circuit established a**</mark><mark style="color:red;">**&#x20;**</mark>_<mark style="color:red;">**per se**</mark>_<mark style="color:red;">**&#x20;**</mark><mark style="color:red;">**rule, holding that any investigatory detention exceeding 20 minutes is automatically unreasonable**</mark>**&#x20;under the Fourth Amendment.**
  * The court reversed the convictions, concluding that Davis's detention had exceeded the permissible limits of a _Terry_ stop.

**Legal Principles and Reasoning:**

* **Reasonable Suspicion Analysis:**
  * **totality of the circumstances**, including the officers' experience and the specific characteristics of the situation.
    * heavy load of the truck,&#x20;
    * the coordinated travel,&#x20;
    * evasive maneuvers of the Pontiac&#x20;
    * diligence of officer actions - not actions intentionally prolonged the stop
  * Thus, TOC sufficient to create a reasonable suspicion of criminal activity.
* **Time Limitation:**
  * The Court explicitly **rejected the Fourth Circuit's&#x20;**_**per se**_**&#x20;20-minute rule**.
  * The Court stressed that the Fourth Amendment requires a **flexible approach, considering the specific facts of each case**.
  * The Court reasoned that **imposing a rigid time limit would unduly restrict law enforcement's ability to conduct effective investigations**.
