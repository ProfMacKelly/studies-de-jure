# Alabama v. White

## FACTS

Officers received an anonymous telephone tip that the defendant would be leaving an apartment complex at a certain time (implied very soon), driving a brown Plymouth station wagon with a broken right taillight lens.&#x20;

The anonymous source stated the defendant would drive to a Dobey's motel and would be in possession of approximately one ounce of cocaine in a brown attaché case.&#x20;

The officers did not know if the anonymous caller was reliable or how the caller knew this information.&#x20;

The officers went to the apartment complex and located a Plymouth station wagon with a broken right taillight in the parking lot. The officers observed the defendant leave the building and enter the station wagon. The officers followed her as she drove to the motel identified by the anonymous source.&#x20;

The officers stopped her. After obtaining the defendant’s consent to search the vehicle, the officers found a locked brown attaché case. The defendant provided the combination to the case and upon opening it the officers found marijuana. The defendant was arrested. During processing, the officers found cocaine in her purse.

## PROC HIST

* White was charged with possession of controlled substances.&#x20;
* She moved to suppress the evidence seized during the vehicle stop and subsequent search, arguing that the anonymous tip did not provide reasonable suspicion to justify the investigatory stop, violating her Fourth Amendment rights.&#x20;
* The trial court denied the motion to suppress.&#x20;
* White was convicted.
* The Alabama Court of Criminal Appeals reversed the trial court's decision, holding that the anonymous tip, even with corroboration, did not provide reasonable suspicion for the stop.&#x20;
* The Alabama Supreme Court denied the State's petition for certiorari.&#x20;

## &#x20;ISSUE

Whether the anonymous tip, as corroborated by independent government observations, was sufficiently reliable so as to give the officers reasonable suspicion for the stop of the defendant?

## &#x20;HELD

Yes; although a "close case"; The corroboration of the anonymous tip by independent police work furnished reasonable suspicion for the stop.

## &#x20;RULE

Totality of the circumstances for informant info includes:

* Reliability
* Basis of knowledge
* Degree of informant's accuracy&#x20;
* By default, anonymous tips are unreliable&#x20;
* Corroborating the anonymous tip by independent police work creates "sufficient indicia of reliability to provide reasonable suspicion" (a la _Terry_)
  * Predictive vs. readily observable facts
* Totality

## DISCUSSION

While the tip provided in this case does not, by itself, give rise to reasonable suspicion, the corroboration of significant aspects of the tip by independent investigation provided the indicia of reliability.&#x20;

* the tipster was able to predict the defendant’s future behavior - “inside information - a special familiarity with the defendant’s affairs” that most members of the general public would not have.&#x20;
* corroboration of much of the tipster’s information gave reason to believe that he was “honest” and “well informed.”

## DISSENT

Millions of people leave their apartments at about the same time every day carrying an attaché case and heading for a destination known to their neighbors. Usually, however, the neighbors do not know what the briefcase contains. An anonymous neighbor’s prediction about somebody’s time of departure and probable destination is anything but a reliable basis for assuming that the commuter is in possession of an illegal substance—particularly when the person is not even carrying the attaché case described by the tipster.
