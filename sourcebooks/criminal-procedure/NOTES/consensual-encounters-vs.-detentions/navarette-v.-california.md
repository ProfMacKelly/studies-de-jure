# Navarette v. California

## FACTS

* **The Anonymous 911 Call:**
  * A 911 dispatcher in Humboldt County, California, received an anonymous call.
  * The caller reported that a silver Ford F-150 pickup truck, license plate number 8D94925, had run her off the road at mile marker 69 on Highway 101.
  * The caller described the incident as having "run them off the roadway."
  * The caller provided the vehicle's make, model, color, and license plate number.
  * The caller indicated the direction in which the truck was traveling.
* **Law Enforcement Response:**
  * The dispatcher relayed the information to California Highway Patrol officers.
  * Approximately 18 minutes later, officers spotted a truck matching the description and license plate number provided by the caller, now at mile marker 65.
  * Officers did not observe any traffic violations or erratic driving by the truck prior to initiating the stop.
* **The Traffic Stop and Discovery:**
  * Officers stopped the vehicle, which was driven by Lorenzo Navarette, with his brother, Jose Navarette, as a passenger.
  * During the stop, officers detected the odor of marijuana emanating from the vehicle.
  * A subsequent search of the truck revealed approximately 30 pounds of marijuana.
  * The Navarette brothers were arrested.

**II. Detailed Procedural History**

* **Trial Court:**
  * The Navarette brothers were charged with transportation of marijuana.
  * They moved to suppress the evidence (the marijuana), arguing that the anonymous 911 call did not provide reasonable suspicion to justify the traffic stop, violating their Fourth Amendment rights.
  * The trial court denied the motion to suppress.
* **California Court of Appeal:**
  * The Navarette brothers appealed the trial court's decision.
  * The California Court of Appeal affirmed the trial court's ruling, holding that the anonymous 911 call provided reasonable suspicion for the traffic stop.
* **California Supreme Court:**
  * The California Supreme Court denied the Navarette brothers' petition for review.
* **United States Supreme Court:**
  * The Navarette brothers petitioned the United States Supreme Court for a writ of certiorari.
  * The Supreme Court granted certiorari to resolve the question of whether the anonymous 911 call provided reasonable suspicion for the traffic stop.

## &#x20;ISSUE

Does an anonymous 911 call, reporting a specific instance of reckless driving, provide reasonable suspicion to justify a traffic stop under the Fourth Amendment, when the caller provides detailed identifying information about the vehicle and reports contemporaneous observations of dangerous conduct?&#x20;

* Specifically, how should the courts weigh the reliability of 911 calls, and how does the concept of contemporaneity effect the reliability of the information provided?

## &#x20;HELD

No. In this case, the traffic stop did not violate the Fourth Amendment because, under the totality of the circumstances, the officer had reasonable suspicion that the driver of the truck was intoxicated.

## RULE

* "Particularized and objective basis for suspecting…criminal activity"&#x20;
* Totality of the circumstances for informant info includes:
  * Reliability
  * Basis of knowledge
  * Degree of informant's accuracy
* By default, anonymous tips are unreliable, but…
  * Corroborating the anonymous tip by independent police work creates "sufficient indicia of reliability to provide reasonable suspicion"
  * Reasonable suspicion also depends on "factual and practical considerations of everyday life on which reasonable and prudent \[people] act"
* **More ... Comprehensive Analysis of the Applicable Rules**
  * **Terry v. Ohio (1968):**
    * Established the "stop and frisk" doctrine, permitting brief investigatory stops when officers have reasonable suspicion, based on specific and articulable facts, that criminal activity may be afoot.
    * Reasonable suspicion is a less demanding standard than probable cause.
  * **Reasonable Suspicion:**
    * Requires specific and articulable facts that, when taken together with rational inferences, warrant a belief that criminal activity may be occurring.
    * The totality of the circumstances must be considered.
    * Anonymous tips can provide reasonable suspicion if they bear adequate indicia of reliability.
  * **Illinois v. Gates (1983):**
    * Established the "totality of the circumstances" analysis for determining probable cause based on hearsay information, including anonymous tips. This methodology is also used when determining reasonable suspicion.
  * **Florida v. J.L. (2000):**
    * Held that an anonymous tip describing a person with a gun, without more, does not provide reasonable suspicion for a stop and frisk.
    * Emphasized the importance of reliability and predictive information in anonymous tips.
    * Distinguished between information that is readily available to anyone, and information that shows inside knowledge.
  * **Alabama v. White (1990):**
    * Held that an anonymous tip, when corroborated by predictive information, can provide reasonable suspicion.
    * Contrasted corroboration of easily observable facts with corroboration of predictive behaviors.
  * **911 Calls:**
    * 911 calls are generally considered more reliable than anonymous tips because they are made under the risk of tracing and prosecution for false reporting.
    * Contemporaneous reports of dangerous driving provide a strong indication of reliability.

## &#x20;DISCUSSION

* Caller described the truck, provided its license plate information and gave the truck’s location to the 911 dispatcher.
* Caller claimed to be an eyewitness (the one who was run off the road) \[basis of knowledge]&#x20;
* Timeline and locations made sense; Police officer located the truck approximately 19 miles away from the scene of the incident, approximately 18 minutes after the 911 call.&#x20;
* And this was a "substantial" event that was in progress
  * Analogous to "excited utterance" hearsay exception
* Use of 911 system was a factor to take into account when determining the reliability of the information she provided. The 911 system had features that allowed for identifying and tracing callers, which would allow a reasonable officer to believe that a person might think twice before calling in a false report
* "factual and practical considerations of everyday life on which reasonable and prudent \[people] act" - being run off the road indicative of being impaired (might not be impaired, but…)
* Other examples
  * Weaving back and forth
  * Crossing center-line
  * Driving in the median

#### Analysis: Even more detail&#x20;

* **Reliability of 911 Calls:**
  * The Supreme Court recognized that 911 calls are inherently more reliable than anonymous tips because of the potential for identification and prosecution for false reporting.
  * The caller's report of being run off the road, a specific and dangerous driving maneuver, added to the call's reliability.
* **Contemporaneity:**
  * The Court emphasized the contemporaneous nature of the caller's report. The caller reported an ongoing dangerous driving incident, which enhanced the reliability of the information.
  * The fact that the caller reported a dangerous driving maneuver that had just occurred, rather than a stale or speculative allegation, indicated a high degree of reliability.
* **Detailed Identifying Information:**
  * The caller provided specific details about the vehicle, including its make, model, color, and license plate number.
  * This level of detail, combined with the contemporaneous report of dangerous driving, provided a strong basis for reasonable suspicion.
* **Nature of the Offense:**
  * The caller reported a specific and dangerous traffic violation, "run them off the roadway," which created a reasonable inference of reckless driving or other hazardous conduct.
  * This is different than the simple assertion of an illegal action, as in Florida v. J.L.
* **Officer's Observations:**
  * While the officers did not observe any traffic violations prior to the stop, the courts have stated that they do not need to observe the violation themselves, if the information provided is reliable.
  * The information provided by the 911 call was reliable enough to justify the stop, even without pre-stop observations of dangerous driving.
* **Totality of the Circumstances:**
  * The Court considered the totality of the circumstances, i**ncluding the reliability of 911 calls, the contemporaneous nature of the report, the detailed identifying information, and the nature of the reported offense.**
  * Based on these factors, the Court concluded that the anonymous 911 call provided reasonable suspicion for the traffic stop.
