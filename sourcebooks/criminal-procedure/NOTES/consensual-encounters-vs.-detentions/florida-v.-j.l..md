# Florida v. J.L.

## Facts

* **The Anonymous Call:**
  * On October 13, 1995, an unidentified individual placed a call to the Dade County, Florida, Police Department.
  * The caller's identity remained completely unknown; no identifying information was provided, such as name, address, or telephone number.
  * The caller reported that a "young black male standing at a particular bus stop" was carrying a gun.
  * The caller further described the individual's clothing, specifically stating he was wearing a "plaid shirt."
  * Crucially, the caller provided no other details:
    * No explanation of how the caller knew the individual possessed a firearm.
    * No predictive information regarding the individual's future actions or intentions.
    * No indication of any imminent danger or threat to public safety beyond the mere assertion of a concealed weapon.
* **Police Response and Observation:**
  * Officers responded to the location of the bus stop identified by the anonymous caller.
  * Upon arrival, officers observed three black males standing at the bus stop.
  * One of the males, J.L., a 15-year-old juvenile, was wearing a plaid shirt, matching the description provided by the caller.
  * Officers observed no other suspicious behavior from J.L. or the other individuals present.
  * No visible firearm was detected by the officers prior to the stop and frisk.
  * The officers had no independent reason to believe any crime was being committed.
* **The Stop and Frisk:**
  * Based solely on the anonymous tip and the corroboration of J.L.'s clothing description, officers initiated a "stop and frisk" of J.L.
  * During the frisk, an officer discovered a concealed firearm in J.L.'s pocket.
  * J.L. was subsequently taken into custody.

## **Procedural History**

* **Trial Court:**
  * J.L. was charged with carrying a concealed firearm without a license and possession of a firearm by a minor.
  * J.L.'s defense moved to suppress the evidence of the firearm, arguing that the search violated his Fourth Amendment right to be free from unreasonable searches and seizures.
  * The trial court granted the motion to suppress, ruling that the anonymous tip did not provide the requisite reasonable suspicion to justify the stop and frisk.
* **Third District Court of Appeal of Florida:**
  * The State of Florida appealed the trial court's suppression order.
  * The Third District Court of Appeal reversed the trial court's decision.
  * The appellate court held that the anonymous tip, as corroborated by the police observation of J.L.'s matching the clothing description, provided sufficient reasonable suspicion for the stop and frisk.
* **Florida Supreme Court:**
  * J.L. appealed the decision of the Third District Court of Appeal to the Florida Supreme Court.
  * The Florida Supreme Court quashed the decision of the District Court of Appeal.
  * The Florida Supreme Court held that the anonymous tip lacked sufficient indicia of reliability to justify a _Terry_ stop.
  * The court emphasized the lack of predictive information and the mere corroboration of readily observable facts.
* **United States Supreme Court:**
  * The State of Florida petitioned the United States Supreme Court for a writ of certiorari.
  * The Supreme Court granted certiorari to resolve the conflict regarding the application of _Terry v. Ohio_ to anonymous tips.

## &#x20;ISSUE

Whether law enforcement officers can base reasonable suspicion solely on an anonymous tip?

* Does an **anonymous tip**, reporting that an individual is carrying a firearm, w**ithout any further indicia of reliability** such as predictive information or a detailed explanation of the basis of knowledge, **provide sufficient reasonable suspicion to justify a police officer's investigatory stop and frisk** under the Fourth Amendment, as articulated in _Terry v. Ohio_?
* Specifically, **does the mere corroboration of readily observable facts, such as an individual's clothing description, satisfy the requirement of reasonable suspicion** **when the tip lacks any predictive information or other corroborating facts that would demonstrate the informant's inside knowledge or reliability?**

## &#x20;HELD

No. Reasonable suspicion must be based on something more than simply an anonymous tip.

* The Supreme Court held that an anonymous tip, reporting that an individual is carrying a firearm, without any further indicia of reliability such as predictive information or a detailed explanation of the basis of knowledge, does not provide sufficient reasonable suspicion to justify a police officer's investigatory stop and frisk under the Fourth Amendment.

## &#x20;RULE

&#x20;_Terry_:

"\[W]here a police officer observes unusual conduct which leads him reasonably to conclude in light of his experience that criminal activity may be afoot and that the persons with whom he is dealing may be armed and presently dangerous, where in the course of investigating this behavior he identifies himself as a policeman and makes reasonable inquiries, and where nothing in the initial stages of the encounter serves to dispel his reasonable fear for his own or others' safety, he is entitled for the protection of himself and others in the area to conduct a carefully limited search of the outer clothing of such persons in an attempt to discover weapons which might be used to assault him.

&#x20;Totality of the circumstances for informant info includes:

* By default, anonymous tips are unreliable&#x20;
* Corroborating the anonymous tip by independent police work creates "sufficient indicia of reliability to provide reasonable suspicion"&#x20;
* Reasonable suspicion to conduct stops and frisks requires that a tip be reliable in its assertion of illegality, not just in its tendency to identify a person.
* **Terry v. Ohio (1968):**
  * Established the "stop and frisk" doctrine, allowing police officers to conduct brief investigatory stops when they have reasonable suspicion, based on specific and articulable facts, that criminal activity may be afoot.
  * Also allows a "frisk" or pat-down for weapons when an officer has reasonable suspicion that the individual may be armed and dangerous.
  * Reasonable suspicion is a less demanding standard than probable cause, but it still requires more than a mere hunch or inchoate suspicion.
* **Reasonable Suspicion:**
  * Requires specific and articulable facts that, when taken together with rational inferences from those facts, warrant a belief that criminal activity may be afoot.
  * The totality of the circumstances must be considered.
  * Mere presence in a high-crime area or matching a general description is not sufficient.
* **Anonymous Tips:**
  * Generally, anonymous tips standing alone are insufficient to establish reasonable suspicion.
  * Reliability is crucial; the tip must provide a basis for believing that the informant has reliable knowledge of criminal activity.
  * Corroboration of readily observable facts, such as location or clothing, does not, by itself, provide sufficient reliability.
  * Predictive information, which demonstrates the informant's inside knowledge or ability to foresee future events, is a significant factor in establishing reliability.
* **Alabama v. White (1990):**
  * Established that an anonymous tip, when corroborated by predictive information, can provide reasonable suspicion.
  * Distinguished between corroboration of readily observable facts and corroboration of predictive behavior.
  * The ability to predict future actions demonstrates a higher degree of knowledge than merely describing the present.

## DISCUSSION

Here, the officer’s suspicion that the defendant was carrying a weapon did not develop from his own observations but solely from a call made from an unknown location by an unknown caller.

&#x20;Tip lacked sufficient indicia of reliability to provide reasonable suspicion to conduct a frisk.

* The tip did not provide predictive information that left the government without means to test the informant’s knowledge or credibility.&#x20;
* No reasonable basis to believe the suspect was engaging in illegal conduct
  * No threatening or unusual movements
  * No observation of firearm
  * Only info came from unknown caller, unknown basis for known; and although the "bus stop" and "plaid shirt" were correct - totality of the circs - not helpful&#x20;
* No reasonable basis to believe the suspect may be armed and presently dangerous
  * No threatening or unusual movements
  * No observation of firearm
  * Only info came from unknown caller, unknown basis for knowledge
