# Virginia v. Moore (2008)

## Facts

* Officers heard on radio that David Lee More was driving with suspended license; an officer knew Moore was known by "Chubs"; they confirm Moore had a suspended license and stopped him. (PC was assumed to exist for the purpose of this case.)
* Virginia law: A suspended license offense should result in an arrest, state law provided that officers should have issued a summons for this offense instead of arrest:
  * "Driving on a suspended license, like some other misdemeanors, is not an arrestable offense except as to those who “fail or refuse to discontinue” the violation, and those whom the officer reasonably believes to be likely to disregard a summons, or likely to harm themselves or others."&#x20;
    * "The intermediate appellate court found none of these circumstances applicable."
* Arrested him (misdemeanor - 1 yr and $2,500 fine)&#x20;
* Subsequent SIA resulted in cocaine and cash&#x20;

## Issue

Whether a police officer violates the Fourth Amendment by making an arrest based on probable cause but prohibited by state law.

## Holding

No

## Rule

When officers have probable cause to believe that a person has committed a crime in their presence, the Fourth Amendment permits them to make an arrest, and to search the suspect in order to safeguard evidence and ensure their own safety

## Analysis

Chubs argues that the Fourth  Amendment incorporates state laws; this is unreasonably given the number and variation of laws government arrest procedures throughout the country&#x20;

This Court has held that when an officer has probable cause to believe a person committed even a minor crime, the arrest is constitutionally reasonable

an arrest based on probable cause serves interests that justify seizure.&#x20;

&#x20;    Arrest:&#x20;

&#x20;    (1) ensures that a suspect appears to answer charges and does not continue a crime, and&#x20;

&#x20;    (2) it safeguards evidence and enables officers to conduct an in-custody investigation

States are free to be more restrictive than the US Constitution, but the 4th Amend needs an "administrable bright-line rule"

A more restrictive policy does not make violations of that policy unreasonable under the 4th Amend
