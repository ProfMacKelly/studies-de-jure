# Atwater v. City of Lago Vista (2001)

## Facts

* Gail Atwater was driving her truck in Lago Vista, Texas, with her children in the front seat.&#x20;
* She and her children were not wearing seatbelts, a violation of Texas law.  &#x20;
  * According to Atwater’s complaint (the allegations of which we assume to be true for present purposes), Turek approached the truck and “yell\[ed]” something to the effect of “\[w]e’ve met before” and “\[y]ou’re going to jail.” He then called for backup and asked to see Atwater’s driver’s license and insurance documentation, which state law required her to carry.&#x20;
  *   When Atwater told Turek that she did not have the papers because her purse had been stolen the day before, Turek said that he had “heard that story two-hundred times.”

      Atwater asked to take her “frightened, upset, and crying” children to a friend’s house nearby, but Turek told her, “\[y]ou’re not going anywhere.”
* Officer Bart Turek stopped her, verbally berated her, and then arrested her.  &#x20;
* She was handcuffed, placed in a squad car, and taken to the police station.  &#x20;
* At the station, she was processed, including having her "mug shot" taken, and placed in a jail cell for about an hour before being taken before a magistrate and released on bond.  &#x20;
* She was charged with violating the seatbelt law, among other offenses.  &#x20;
* She pleaded no contest to the seatbelt misdemeanors and paid a $50 fine.  &#x20;
* Atwater and her husband sued the city, alleging that the arrest violated her Fourth Amendment right to be free from unreasonable seizure. &#x20;
* Texas law permitted warrantless arrest but permitted officers to issue citations instead&#x20;

## Issue&#x20;

Whether it violates the 4th Amendment to arrest someone for a minor misdemeanor offense punishable only by a fine&#x20;

* Atwater wanted rule forbidding this when a conviction could not ultimately carry any jail time and when the government shows no compelling need for immediate detention.&#x20;

## Holding

No; even for a minor misdemeanor, including a fine-only traffic offense, an arrest does not violate the 4th Amend&#x20;

## Rule

* Probable cause “applie\[s] to all arrests; if an officer has probable cause to believe that an individual has committed even a very minor criminal offense in his presence, he may, without violating the Fourth Amendment, arrest the offender."

## \[Analysis]

* Broad, historical common law history indicates arrest authority to include petty/misdemeanors
  * Jurisdictions that have permitted warrantless arrests—including private citizen's arrests—permit arrests for many minor offenses
  * No evidence from the "framing era" that arrest authority would have excluded misdemeanors
* no need to require officer to balance the need for arrest against the severity of the offense



## DISSENT

no state interest in this "pointless indignity"

the 4th amendment requires reasonableness

the full extent of liberty and privacy issues that occur during an after an arrest does not serve the purposes of arrest procedures — does not serve state interests in custodial arrests
