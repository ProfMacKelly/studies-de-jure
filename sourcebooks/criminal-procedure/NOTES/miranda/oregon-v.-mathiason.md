# Oregon v. Mathiason

Here is a case brief for _Oregon v. Mathiason_, drawing upon the information in the provided sources:

**Oregon v. Carl Ray Mathiason** **Citation:** 429 U.S. 492 (1977) **Court:** Supreme Court of the United States

**Facts:**

* Carl Ray Mathiason was convicted of first-degree burglary after a bench trial.
* His confession was critical to the State’s case.
* An officer investigating a theft asked the victim if she suspected anyone, and she named Mathiason, a parolee and associate of her son.
* The officer left his card with a note asking Mathiason to call because he wanted to discuss something.
* Mathiason called the next afternoon and **agreed to meet the officer at the state patrol office**. The office was about two blocks from his apartment and housed several state agencies.
* The officer met Mathiason in the hallway, shook hands, and **took him into an office, closing the door.**
* They sat across a desk, and a police radio could be heard from another room.
* Mathiason was **told he was** **not under arrest**.
* The officer stated he wanted to **talk about the burglary, that truthfulness might be considered by the district attorney or judge, and that police believed Mathiason was involved**.
* The officer **falsely stated** that Mathiason’s fingerprints were found at the scene.
* Within five minutes of arriving, Mathiason stated he had taken the property (confessed).
* Only **after** he confessed did the officer advise Mathiason of his _Miranda_ rights and take a taped confession.
* After the taped confession, the officer told Mathiason he was **not arresting him at that time** and released him to go about his job and return to his family.
* Mathiason left the office after about a half-hour interview.
* The officer referred the case to the district attorney.

**Procedural History:**

* Mathiason moved to suppress the confession at trial, arguing it was the fruit of questioning by the police not preceded by the warnings required by _Miranda v. Arizona_.
* The trial court **refused to exclude the confession**, finding that Mathiason was not in custody at the time of the confession.
* The Oregon Court of Appeals **affirmed** Mathiason’s conviction.
* The Supreme Court of Oregon, by a divided vote, **reversed the conviction**. It found that although Mathiason had not been arrested or formally detained, the interrogation took place in a “coercive environment” of the sort to which _Miranda_ was intended to apply.
* The State of Oregon petitioned for certiorari to the Supreme Court of the United States.

**Issue:** **Did the questioning of Mathiason at the police station, under the circumstances described, constitute "custodial interrogation" requiring&#x20;**_**Miranda**_**&#x20;warnings?**

**Holding:** No. The Supreme Court held that Mathiason was **not in custody**, and therefore, _Miranda_ warnings were not required before he made his initial confession. The judgment of the Supreme Court of Oregon was **reversed**.

**Reasoning (Per Curiam - Majority):**

* The rules set forth in _Miranda_ apply to "**custodial interrogation**".
* Custodial interrogation is defined as "**questioning initiated by law enforcement officers after a person has been taken into custody or otherwise deprived of his freedom of action in any significant way".**
* Previous cases have applied the _Miranda_ principle to questioning in a prison setting or in a suspect's home after arrest, where they are no longer free to go.
* However, in the present case, **there was no indication that Mathiason's freedom to depart was restricted in any way**.
* He **came voluntarily** to the police station.
* He was **immediately informed that he was not under arrest**.
* He **did in fact leave** the police station without hindrance at the close of the interview.
* Based on these facts, Mathiason was clearly not in custody "or otherwise deprived of his freedom of action in any significant way".
* A situation that is not custodial is **not converted** to a custodial one simply because a reviewing court concludes that the questioning took place in a "**coercive environment**," even if there was no formal arrest or restraint.
* **Any interview** of a suspect by a police officer will have **coercive aspects** simply because the officer is part of a system that may lead to charges.
* Police officers **are not required** to administer _Miranda_ warnings to **everyone they question**.
* Warnings are **not required** simply because questioning occurs at the **station house** or because the person questioned is a **suspect**.
* _Miranda_ warnings are required **only** where there has been such a **restriction on a person’s freedom** as to render him "in custody".
* The **coercive environment** that _Miranda_ addressed and is limited to is one where the person's freedom is significantly restricted, not merely the inherent pressure of a police interview.
* The officer's false statement about fingerprints, while potentially relevant to other issues, **does not affect whether Mathiason was in custody** for _Miranda_ purposes.

**Reasoning (Marshall, J., dissenting):**

* Justice Marshall dissented, arguing that a determination of whether Mathiason was significantly deprived of his freedom was **not possible on the record**.
* He contended that **formalities alone cannot control**.
* At a minimum, if Mathiason had an **objectively reasonable belief** that he was not free to leave, then he was deprived of his freedom of action in a significant way.
* Plainly, Mathiason could have reasonably believed this, especially after being told the police believed he was involved and that his fingerprints were found at the scene.
* The dissent disagreed more fundamentally with the Court's conclusion that **if the respondent were not in custody, no warnings were required**.
* While acknowledging _Miranda_ is limited to custodial interrogations, the dissent argued this was because the facts in the _Miranda_ cases raised only that "narrow issue".
* In Justice Marshall's view, even if Mathiason were not in custody, the **coercive elements** in this case were **so pervasive as to require&#x20;**_**Miranda**_**-type warnings**.
* **These coercive elements included: being interrogated in "privacy" and "unfamiliar surroundings" (factors&#x20;**_**Miranda**_**&#x20;stressed), the investigation having focused on Mathiason, and the use of "deceptive stratagems" like the false fingerprint statement.**
* The dissent agreed with the Oregon Supreme Court that excusing the absence of warnings under these facts is "contrary to the rationale expressed in _Miranda_".
* Justice Marshall argued that the privilege against self-incrimination should be as broad as the mischief it guards against, and the Court's decision meant the Fifth Amendment privilege does not provide full protection against mischiefs equivalent to, but different from, custodial interrogation.
