## **Summary of&#x20;**_**Devenpeck v. Alford**_**&#x20;(2004)**

**Core Issue:**\
Whether an arrest violates the Fourth Amendment when the crime for which probable cause exists is _different from_ (and not “closely related” to) the offense the officer cites at the moment of arrest.

***

### **Background**

* A Washington State Patrol officer suspected Alford of **impersonating a police officer** after seeing him assisting motorists with wig‑wag headlights and police‑style equipment.
* During the stop, officers discovered Alford was **recording the encounter** and arrested him for violating the **Washington Privacy Act**.
* That charge was later dismissed.
* Alford sued under §1983, claiming **unlawful arrest without probable cause**.

***

### **Lower Court Proceedings**

* Jury found for the officers.
* The Ninth Circuit reversed, holding:
  * Officers lacked probable cause for the Privacy Act violation.
  * Probable cause for other offenses (impersonation, obstruction) was irrelevant because they were not “closely related” to the stated arrest offense.

***

### **Supreme Court Holding**

#### **1. The “closely related offense” rule is unconstitutional.**

* The Court rejected the Ninth Circuit’s rule that probable cause must relate to the specific offense the officer names at arrest.
* **Probable cause is an objective standard**:\
  If the facts known to the officer support probable cause for _any_ offense, the arrest is valid.
* An officer’s **subjective intent or stated reason** is irrelevant.

#### **2. Remand for probable‑cause determination.**

* The Court did not decide whether probable cause existed for impersonation or obstruction.
* It sent the case back because the Ninth Circuit never evaluated those grounds.

***

### **Key Principles Clarified**

* **Objective reasonableness governs** Fourth Amendment analysis.
* Officers are **not required** to articulate the correct offense at the moment of arrest.
* The Court warned that the Ninth Circuit’s rule would:
  * Encourage officers to list every possible offense, or
  * Discourage them from giving any reason at all.
